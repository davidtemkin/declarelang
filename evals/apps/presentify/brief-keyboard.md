# Presentify: the keyboard

People who spend hours in Presentify want to keep their hands on the keyboard. Make everything it does reachable
and fast from the keyboard alone, as in the best desktop tools, without changing anything that works with the
pointer. Everything the first brief asks for must keep working.

## 1. Getting around

- **Tab** moves through the window's parts in the order they are read: the toolbar, the slide list, the slide, the
  inspector. Within a part, Tab goes from control to control in the order they appear on screen, and Shift-Tab goes
  back.
- **The slide list** is one stop: the arrow keys move between slides, Home and End go to the first and last, and
  the slide shown follows. Option-Up and Option-Down move the current slide up or down the list.
- **The slide** is one stop. With it focused, the person can select each thing on it in turn, and Return starts
  editing a selected text box. Escape ends editing, then clears the selection.
- Where the focus is always shows, clearly, on any colour, when the keyboard put it there.

## 2. Doing things

Every command in the app has a key, shown beside it wherever it appears in a menu. At least:

- **Tools:** V select, R rectangle, O ellipse, L line, T text. A shape or text box can also be added from the
  keyboard alone: it appears at a default size in the middle of the slide, selected.
- **Editing:** cut, copy, paste, duplicate, delete, select all, undo, redo — the usual keys.
- **Arranging:** group and ungroup; bring forward, send backward, bring to front, send to back; the six aligns and
  the two distributes.
- **Slides:** new slide, duplicate slide, delete slide.
- **Presenting:** start presenting from the current slide, and from the first.
- **Style:** bold, italic, larger and smaller text, and left, centre and right alignment, for a selected text box
  or while typing in one.

A key does what the person means where the focus is: Delete while typing deletes a character, not the text box;
Command-A while typing selects the text, not every shape; the arrow keys in a field of the inspector change the
field, not the selection on the slide.

## 3. The inspector

Every value in the inspector can be reached and changed from the keyboard: a number by typing, or step by step
with the arrow keys (by ten with Shift); a colour by choosing one of its swatches with the arrow keys; a choice
by the arrow keys. A change applies when the person presses Return or leaves the field, is one step to undo, and
Escape in a field puts back what was there.

## 4. Menus and dialogs

- Every menu and pop-up opens from the keyboard, is moved through with the arrow keys and typed letters, picks
  with Return, and closes with Escape, the focus going back where it was.
- Every dialog — deleting a presentation, a slide changed in another window — answers Return with its main choice
  and Escape with its cancel, and keeps the focus inside it while it is open.

## 5. Learning the keys

The person can see every shortcut in one place, grouped as the menus are, without leaving what they are doing, and
each command's key shows wherever the command does.

## 6. What done looks like

Without touching the pointer, someone opens Presentify, starts a new presentation, adds three slides, puts a
title and two shapes on the first, aligns the shapes, styles the title, reorders the slides, undoes the reorder,
and presents from the start — then does it again faster, having learned the keys from the app itself.
